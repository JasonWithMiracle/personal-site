Over the past two years I've grown more convinced of one thing: **simplicity is a kind of advanced complexity**.

Many products start light and clean. Then, to "serve more needs," buttons multiply and settings get deeper, until the thing becomes a monster nobody wants to open. The complexity never went away — it was just pushed onto the user.

## Hide the complexity well

Good design doesn't eliminate complexity; it tucks it away neatly. Like a good camera: pro mode hidden in a corner, auto mode always in front.

> Users don't want more switches. They want fewer decisions.

## Restraint is a stance

Saying "no" is far harder than saying "yes." Every feature adds future maintenance cost and present cognitive load. So my habit now: think about why *not* to build it, before why to build it.

Simple was never cheap.
